# Blog posts and project pages

A blog post is a high-level tour of the paper for a broad reader. It keeps the paper's findings and names but talks
to "you".

- About 1,000 words. A teaser figure, then a one-line Tl;dr, then a short contents list.
- Each section: a question, one finding with one number, one or two sentences of intuition, then "You can explore
  ... below" and an interactive figure.
- Method only as far as a reader needs to understand the finding.
- Use the paper's terms and explain each at its first use. A concept that the paper later splits is introduced whole.
- Open the background with the human case, then map it onto the system.
- A contrast that draws on the reader's intuition opens with "you", in varied forms, at most once per section:
  "If you were in charge, you would probably ...", "You may have heard the advice ...", "You might guess ...",
  "If you had to fix it, your first idea would likely be ...".
- A playful aside is allowed now and then.
- The implications subsections end on questions. The post ends on a quote.
- Interactive figures show the same running example as the paper, with a switch to compare the success case.
