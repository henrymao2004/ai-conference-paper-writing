# Figures, tables, captions and boxes

## Captions

A caption tells the result before the reader looks at the figure.

1. A bold first sentence with the finding, worded like the matching finding box.
2. At most one or two sentences the reader needs to read the figure: what a panel, row, symbol or marked column
   stands for.
3. The uncertainty with its n inside it, as one short sentence: "Rates are in percent with ±1 SD over 80 tasks."

Leave out:

- "We test whether ..." sentences that restate the bold sentence.
- How the experiment was run. That goes in the section's paragraph. Until that paragraph exists, keep the sentence as
  a `% Paragraph to write:` comment above the float.
- Standalone sample sizes. A count stays only inside the uncertainty it defines.
- Notes, caveats, provenance ("values come from ...") and parentheticals.
- Descriptions of colors, shading or the grid: "Cells give ...", "Each cell shows ...", "green marks ...". Say what
  the numbers show.
- "Share of ...", "The bars give ...". Say the finding.

A caption that mentions a quote or a case says what it actually contains, who wrote it and what it claims.

Aim for about 20 to 45 words.

## Labels inside figures and tables

- Titles, axes, legends, chips and table rows say the observable thing in plain words: what a text literally does
  ("Copies the context", "Answers without the source"), what a condition literally gives ("Question only",
  "Question and the source", "Two worked examples").
- Never category names, data ids, code names or coined condition labels.
- Full role names in keys, never clipped words.
- Labels fit their column. Shorten the wording before shrinking the font or letting text run over bars.

## Figure design

- Pick the chart by the comparison: dumbbell for before and after per group, arrows for a change, flows for
  transitions, scatter for two measures, taxonomy rows for categories. Bars only for a few shares.
- One idea per plot. Panel order follows the text.
- Each condition keeps one color across every figure. Two conditions share a hue family only when no figure shows
  both.
- Put names on the marks so the legend is one short row.
- Every panel has a reference and the change against it.
- Axes hug the data. Uncertainty as bands or error bars, ±1 SD unless the paper says otherwise.
- Vector PDF, embedded fonts. Check the rendered figure for overlap before calling it done.

## Tables

- Grouped rows, bold group names, the reference row first.
- Small gray ± for uncertainty and a signed change against the reference, colored by direction when that helps.
- Best value bold, second underlined, when ranking matters.
- No notes inside cells or captions.

## Finding boxes

A short box at the start of a results subsection: number, italic topic, bold one-sentence finding. The box and the
caption of the subsection's main figure share the same sentence.

## Teaser

A concept picture of the running example on top and the main results below. Every text in it follows the wording
rules: what each agent or person literally received and wrote, no system mechanics, no failure-colored styling on
text that should look like a success.
